The old chapel stands at the quiet edge of Willowbrook, its whitewashed clapboard gone soft and gray with age, its steeple leaning ever so gently toward the pines. Built by the town's founding families more than a century ago, it was once the beating heart of the community—the place where vows were spoken, grief was carried, and hymns rose through the rafters on Sunday mornings. In the years of Grace's absence it fell into neglect, the roof failing and the pews gathering dust, until it seemed destined to be forgotten entirely. Yet the chapel holds the memory of the town like a keepsake, and its slow, patient restoration becomes the mirror of Grace's own return: a reminder that what is broken is not always beyond mending, and that grace, like an open door, waits longer than we deserve.
