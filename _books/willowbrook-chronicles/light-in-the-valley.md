Grace Bennett hadn't set foot in Willowbrook since the summer everything fell apart. Now, with her mother gone and a house full of memories to sort through, she has no choice but to return to the Georgia town that shaped and scarred her. She tells herself it will be quick—sign the papers, sell the house, and leave the past where it belongs.

But Willowbrook has a way of holding on. Between the peeling paint of the old chapel and the steady kindness of Thomas Bennett, a widowed carpenter with griefs of his own, Grace begins to feel the slow thaw of a heart she'd kept frozen for years. As buried truths surface and old wounds ask to be tended, she must decide whether the faith of her childhood was something she lost—or something that was waiting, patient and unhurried, for her to come home.
